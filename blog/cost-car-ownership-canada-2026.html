The Real Cost of Car Ownership in Canada (2026)

Rick Minji•March 12, 2026•13 min read

Canadians love to talk about their car payments but rarely calculate the total cost of ownership. When you add insurance, fuel, maintenance, parking, and the silent killer — depreciation — owning a car costs far more than most people realize. We've crunched every number so you can see exactly where your money goes.

🎯 Key Takeaway

  • The average new car in Canada costs $12,000-$16,000 per year to own — that's $1,000-$1,333/month, all-in
  • Depreciation is the biggest single cost — a $45K vehicle loses ~$7,000-$9,000 in year one alone
  • Insurance varies wildly: $1,200/year in Quebec vs $2,400+/year in Ontario
  • EVs save $1,500-$2,500/year on fuel and maintenance but cost more upfront
  • A 3-year-old used car can save you $4,000-$6,000/year vs buying new

1. Purchase or Lease Payment

The average new vehicle transaction price in Canada hit $48,000 in 2025. Even with rates coming down from their 2023-2024 peaks, financing costs remain significant.

Financing a New Vehicle ($45,000)

  • 7-year loan at 5.49%: $649/month → $7,788/year
  • 5-year loan at 4.99%: $849/month → $10,188/year
  • Total interest paid (7-year): ~$9,500
  • Total interest paid (5-year): ~$5,900

Leasing ($45,000 MSRP)

  • 4-year lease at 4.99%: ~$480-$550/month → $5,760-$6,600/year
  • Plus disposition fee ($300-$500) at end of lease
  • Excess mileage charges: $0.12-$0.20/km over limit
  • You don't own the vehicle — no equity at the end
💡The Sweet Spot: 2-3 Year Old Used

A 2-3 year old vehicle has already taken the biggest depreciation hit (30-40%) but still has manufacturer warranty remaining. A $45K new car might cost $28-32K at 3 years old — saving you $13-17K in purchase price while getting essentially the same vehicle. This is the single biggest money-saving move in car ownership.

3. Fuel vs EV Charging Costs

Gas Vehicle (15,000 km/year)

  • Average fuel economy: 8.5 L/100km (mid-size sedan)
  • Annual fuel consumption: 1,275 litres
  • At $1.55/L (national average): $1,976/year
  • SUV at 11 L/100km: $2,558/year

Electric Vehicle (15,000 km/year)

  • Average efficiency: 17 kWh/100km
  • Annual consumption: 2,550 kWh
  • Home charging at $0.13/kWh (Ontario off-peak): $332/year
  • Home charging at $0.08/kWh (Quebec): $204/year
  • Public fast charging at $0.35/kWh: $893/year
  • Blended (80% home / 20% public): ~$440/year
📌Fuel Savings: $1,500+ Per Year

Switching from a gas sedan to an EV saves approximately $1,500-$1,700/year in fuel costs if you mostly charge at home. In Quebec, the savings are even larger (~$1,750) thanks to the cheapest electricity in North America. Factor in lower maintenance costs (no oil changes, brake pads last 2-3x longer), and the total operating cost advantage of EVs is $2,000-$3,000/year.

4. Maintenance and Repairs

Gas Vehicle (Annual Average)

  • Oil changes (3x/year): $250
  • Tire rotation + balance: $80
  • Winter tire swap (2x/year): $100
  • Brake pads (every 50K km): $150/year amortized
  • Tire replacement (every 60K km): $200/year amortized
  • Unexpected repairs (average): $400-$800
  • Total: $1,180-$1,580/year (newer vehicle, rising with age)

Electric Vehicle (Annual Average)

  • Tire rotation: $80
  • Cabin air filter: $50
  • Wiper blades + washer fluid: $40
  • Brake service (much less frequent): $50/year amortized
  • Tire replacement (slightly faster due to weight): $250/year amortized
  • Total: $470-$650/year

5. Parking, Registration, and Fees

  • Vehicle registration: $75-$250/year (varies by province)
  • Driver's licence renewal: $25-$90/year (amortized)
  • Parking (suburban, home driveway): $0
  • Parking (downtown Toronto/Vancouver): $2,400-$4,800/year for a monthly spot
  • Occasional paid parking: $300-$600/year
  • Highway 407/tolls (Ontario): $0-$2,000/year depending on usage
  • Estimated average: $400-$1,200/year

6. Depreciation (The Hidden Killer)

Depreciation is the single biggest cost of car ownership — and most people completely ignore it because it doesn't show up as a monthly bill.

Average Depreciation Curve

  • Year 1: 15-20% loss ($45K → $36-38K) = $7,000-$9,000
  • Year 2: 12-15% loss = $4,500-$5,700
  • Year 3: 10-12% loss = $3,600-$4,300
  • Years 4-5: 8-10% per year = $2,500-$3,500/year
  • 5-year total depreciation on a $45K vehicle: approximately $20,000-$25,000

Best and Worst for Depreciation

  • Hold value well: Toyota, Lexus, Porsche, Jeep Wrangler, trucks (Tacoma, Tundra)
  • Depreciate fast: Luxury sedans (BMW 5/7 series, Mercedes S-Class), some EVs with rapid model changes, Nissan, Dodge

Total Annual Cost Breakdown

Let's put it all together for a $45,000 gas sedan, financed over 5 years, driven 15,000 km/year in Ontario:

📌That's $1,372/Month — All In

Most Canadians think their car costs $849/month (the payment). The real cost is closer to $1,372/month when you account for everything. Over 5 years, this $45K sedan actually costs you roughly $82,000. That's the kind of math that makes you think twice about new vs used, or whether you really need a car at all.

The Bottom Line

  • New cars are expensive. $12,000-$16,000/year all-in is typical. Most people underestimate by 40-50%.
  • Buy used (2-3 years old). Let someone else pay for the steepest depreciation curve. Savings: $4,000-$6,000/year.
  • Consider an EV if you can charge at home. Operating savings of $2,000-$3,000/year offset the higher purchase price over time.
  • Insurance matters. Shop around annually — switching insurers saves the average Canadian $300-$500/year.
  • Don't forget depreciation. It's the biggest single cost and it's completely invisible until you sell.
  • Run the full math. Before buying any vehicle, calculate the total annual cost — not just the monthly payment.

🎯 Cost-Saving Checklist

  • ✅ Buy 2-3 years used instead of new
  • ✅ Choose a vehicle known for holding value (Toyota, Honda, Hyundai)
  • ✅ Shop insurance annually — compare at least 3 providers
  • ✅ Consider EV if you have home charging access
  • ✅ Budget for the TOTAL cost, not just the payment
