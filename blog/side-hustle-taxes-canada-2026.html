Side Hustle Taxes in Canada: What You Owe on Every Extra Dollar

Rick Minji•March 24, 2026•12 min read

You drove for Uber on weekends, sold crafts on Etsy, picked up some freelance design work, or rented out your basement suite. The money hit your bank account and it felt great — until tax season arrived. Now you're staring at a T1 wondering: do I actually owe tax on this?

Short answer: yes. In Canada, every dollar of income is taxable, regardless of whether it came from your employer, a side gig, or a one-time freelance project. There is no minimum threshold below which side income is tax-free. The CRA expects you to report it all.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • All side hustle income is taxable — there's no $600 threshold in Canada (that's a US rule)
  • You must register for GST/HST once you earn $30,000+ in four consecutive calendar quarters
  • Report self-employment income on Form T2125 (Statement of Business Activities) attached to your T1
  • You can deduct legitimate business expenses — home office, supplies, vehicle, software — to reduce taxable income
  • If you owe $3,000+ in net tax, the CRA may require quarterly installment payments
  • Penalties for not reporting include 5% of unpaid tax + 1% per month (up to 12 months), plus interest
  • Side hustle net income creates RRSP contribution room but does not count as pensionable employment for CPP unless you opt in

When Does Side Income Become Taxable?

Immediately. Unlike the United States, which has a $600 reporting threshold for platforms (1099-K), Canada has no minimum. If you earned $50 selling handmade candles at a craft fair, that $50 is technically taxable income.

The CRA distinguishes between two types of non-employment income:

  • Business income: You're running an activity with a reasonable expectation of profit. This is reported on T2125. Most side hustles fall here.
  • Hobby income: No reasonable expectation of profit. The CRA generally won't tax pure hobbies — but if you're making money consistently, it's a business in their eyes.
⚠️The "Hobby" Defence Is Weak

If you're selling products regularly, advertising your services, or have repeat customers, the CRA will consider it a business — even if you don't think of it that way. The test is whether you have a reasonable expectation of profit, not whether you've incorporated or registered a business name.

What About Selling Personal Items?

Selling your old couch on Facebook Marketplace? That's not taxable — it's a disposition of personal property. But if you're buying items specifically to resell at a profit (flipping), that's business income. The line is intent: are you selling stuff you're done with, or are you running a resale operation?

GST/HST Registration: The $30,000 Threshold

If your gross revenue from taxable supplies exceeds $30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters, you must register for a GST/HST account with the CRA. This is mandatory, not optional.

What This Means in Practice

  • Under $30K: You're a "small supplier" — no need to charge or remit GST/HST. You can voluntarily register if you want to claim Input Tax Credits (ITCs) on your business purchases.
  • Over $30K: You must register, charge GST/HST on your services/products, file GST/HST returns (annually, quarterly, or monthly), and remit the collected tax.
  • Ride-share and accommodation: Uber, Lyft, and short-term rental (Airbnb) drivers must register for GST/HST regardless of revenue — the $30K small supplier exemption does not apply.
📌Quick Start Method

If your annual taxable revenue is under $400,000, you can use the Quick Method of accounting for GST/HST. Instead of tracking every ITC, you remit a reduced percentage of your revenue (varies by province, typically 3.6%–8.8%). For many side hustlers, this is simpler and can actually save money.

Common Side Hustles and Their Tax Treatment

Rideshare (Uber, Lyft)

  • Income reported on T2125 as business income
  • Must register for GST/HST immediately — no $30K exemption
  • Deductible expenses: vehicle costs (gas, insurance, maintenance, depreciation), phone plan, car washes
  • Keep a mileage log — CRA requires it to claim vehicle expenses

Etsy / Online Selling

  • All sales revenue is business income on T2125
  • GST/HST registration required once you cross $30K
  • Deductible: materials, packaging, shipping, Etsy fees, photography equipment, home office
  • Inventory costs are deductible when sold, not when purchased

Freelancing (Design, Writing, Consulting, Development)

  • Business income on T2125
  • GST/HST applies once over $30K
  • Deductible: software subscriptions, hardware, home office, professional development, subcontractors
  • If clients pay you in USD, you must convert to CAD using the Bank of Canada exchange rate on the date of payment (or use the annual average rate)

Rental Income

  • Reported on Form T776 (Statement of Real Estate Rentals), not T2125
  • Long-term residential rentals are exempt from GST/HST
  • Short-term rentals (Airbnb, under 30 consecutive days) require GST/HST registration regardless of revenue
  • Deductible: mortgage interest (not principal), property tax, insurance, repairs, utilities, property management fees
💡Track Everything From Day One

Open a separate bank account for your side hustle income. Use a simple spreadsheet or an app like Wave (free, Canadian) to track income and expenses. The CRA can reassess up to 3 years back (6 years if they suspect negligence), so keep records for at least 6 years.

Deductible Expenses: What You Can Write Off

This is where side hustlers leave the most money on the table. Every legitimate business expense reduces your taxable income dollar-for-dollar. Common deductions include:

Home Office (Business-Use-of-Home)

  • Calculate the percentage of your home used for business (by square footage or number of rooms)
  • Apply that percentage to: rent or mortgage interest, utilities, home insurance, property tax, internet, maintenance
  • The space must be your principal place of business or used exclusively for earning business income and meeting clients
  • Home office expenses cannot create or increase a business loss — carry them forward instead

Vehicle Expenses

  • Two methods: actual costs (gas, insurance, maintenance, CCA) × business-use percentage, or the CRA per-kilometre rate (70¢/km for the first 5,000 km, 64¢/km after in 2026)
  • You must keep a mileage log — no log means no deduction
  • CCA (depreciation) for passenger vehicles is capped at $37,000 + tax for 2026

Other Common Deductions

  • Supplies and materials: Anything consumed in producing your product or service
  • Software and subscriptions: Adobe Creative Cloud, accounting software, domain hosting
  • Professional fees: Accountant, legal advice, business registration
  • Advertising: Website costs, social media ads, business cards
  • Phone and internet: Business-use percentage of your personal plans
  • Meals and entertainment: 50% deductible when directly related to earning business income
  • Capital cost allowance (CCA): Depreciation on equipment — laptop, camera, tools
⚠️Don't Get Creative With Deductions

The CRA audits self-employment returns at a higher rate than salaried T4 returns. Claim only expenses that are directly related to earning business income. Your Netflix subscription is not a business expense just because you "need to relax after working." Keep receipts for everything.

How to Report Side Hustle Income on Your T1

Self-employment income goes on Form T2125: Statement of Business or Professional Activities. Here's the process:

  1. Complete T2125 with your gross income, expenses by category, and net income (or loss)
  2. The net income flows to line 13500 (gross self-employment income) and line 13700 (net self-employment income) on your T1
  3. CPP contributions: As a self-employed person, you pay both the employee and employer portions of CPP on your net self-employment income. In 2026, that's 11.9% on income between $3,500 and $71,300 (plus CPP2 on earnings up to $81,200)
  4. No EI: Self-employment income is not subject to Employment Insurance premiums (unless you opt in for special benefits)

Filing Deadline

If you or your spouse/partner are self-employed, your T1 filing deadline extends to June 15. However, any tax owing is still due April 30. Filing late when you owe triggers penalties and interest from May 1.

Quarterly Tax Installments

If your net tax owing exceeds $3,000 in the current year and either of the two preceding years, the CRA will ask you to make quarterly installment payments. This is common for side hustlers whose income grows.

How It Works

  • The CRA sends installment reminders with suggested amounts in February and August
  • Payments are due: March 15, June 15, September 15, and December 15
  • Three calculation methods: no-change (based on last year), prior-year, or current-year estimate
  • If you don't pay installments when required, the CRA charges installment interest — even if you pay the full balance by April 30
💡Set Aside 25–30% as You Earn

A simple rule: every time side hustle income hits your account, immediately transfer 25–30% to a separate savings account earmarked for tax. This covers income tax + CPP contributions. You'll thank yourself in April.

Penalties for Not Reporting Side Income

The CRA is increasingly able to match income reported by platforms (Uber, Airbnb, Etsy) with your tax return. Here's what happens if you don't report:

Late-Filing Penalty

  • 5% of unpaid tax at the filing deadline, plus 1% per month for up to 12 months
  • If you've been penalized before, the rate doubles: 10% + 2% per month (up to 20 months)

Failure to Report Income

  • If you fail to report income on your return and have done so in any of the three preceding tax years, the CRA can assess a federal penalty of 10% of the unreported amount
  • Provincial penalties may apply on top of that

Gross Negligence

  • If the CRA determines you knowingly failed to report income: penalty of 50% of the tax owing on the unreported amount
  • In extreme cases, tax evasion is a criminal offence with fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and potential imprisonment
📌Voluntary Disclosures Program (VDP)

If you've failed to report side income in past years, the CRA's Voluntary Disclosures Program lets you come forward before they find you. If accepted, you'll pay the tax and interest owing but may avoid penalties and prosecution. The earlier you disclose, the better the outcome.

TFSA & RRSP Implications

RRSP Contribution Room

Here's the good news: net self-employment income (line 13700 on your T1) creates RRSP contribution room — 18% of your earned income, up to the annual maximum ($32,490 for 2026). This is in addition to any room from employment income.

If you earned $20,000 from your side hustle after expenses, that generates $3,600 in new RRSP room the following year. Contributing to your RRSP then reduces your taxable income, effectively recovering some of the tax paid on your side hustle earnings.

TFSA

TFSA contribution room is based on age (18+), not income, so side hustle earnings don't directly affect your TFSA room. However, the extra income gives you more cash to actually contribute. Growing your side hustle income inside a TFSA means future investment gains are completely tax-free.

CPP Contributions and Retirement

Self-employed individuals pay both the employee and employer share of CPP. While that feels painful (11.9% in 2026), it does build your CPP retirement pension. If your combined employment + self-employment income is already above the maximum pensionable earnings ($71,300 base + CPP2 ceiling of $81,200), your self-employment CPP obligation may be reduced or eliminated.

The Bottom Line

Side hustles are a fantastic way to build wealth, pay down debt, or fund your TFSA and RRSP. But the CRA expects their share, and ignorance isn't a defence. Here's your action plan:

  • Report all side income — there's no minimum threshold in Canada
  • Track expenses religiously — they reduce your taxable income dollar-for-dollar
  • Set aside 25–30% of gross income for taxes as you earn it
  • Register for GST/HST if you're over $30K (or in ride-share/short-term rentals at any revenue)
  • File T2125 with your T1 return — the self-employment filing deadline is June 15, but tax is due April 30
  • Use your new RRSP room to shelter some of that side income from future tax
  • Consider an accountant once your side hustle earns $10K+ — the fee pays for itself in deductions you'd otherwise miss
